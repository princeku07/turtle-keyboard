Design & Product Brief: Turtle Keyboard (turtlekeyboard.com)
1. Deep-Dive Product Context (What We Are Building)
The Core Problem: Mobile Context-Switching Fatigue
On desktop, users have terminal command lines, Raycast, Alfred, and browser extensions to navigate work and AI without friction. On mobile, the workflow is fundamentally broken. To schedule a task, look up a fact, generate an image, or create a poll during a chat, a user must leave their active app, open a separate AI or utility app, copy the output, switch back to the chat, and paste it. This constantly interrupts human flow and conversation.

The Solution: Turtle Keyboard
Turtle Keyboard is an open-source (MIT licensed), cross-platform (iOS + Android) smart keyboard that turns the mobile text field into a universal command line and interactive application platform.

The Slash Trigger (/): By typing a / in any app (WhatsApp, Slack, Gmail, Tinder, iMessage), Turtle intercepts the input and displays a sleek, floating menu of tools and AI actions right above the keyboard.

Interactive Outputs (MCP Apps): Instead of just dumping blocks of text, Turtle leverages the Model Context Protocol (MCP) to generate live, shareable micro-apps. If a user types /poll Where should we go for dinner?, Turtle generates a lightweight, interactive web widget link directly in the chat. Recipients tap it to vote in real-time within a beautiful, sandboxed viewer.

The Privacy Moat (Local-First): Keyboards are inherently sensitive real estate. Users rightfully fear third-party keyboards acting as keyloggers. Turtle solves this by utilizing on-device local AI inference. Standard processing happens directly on the user's hardware—meaning private chats and passwords never hit a cloud server.

2. Target Audience & User Personas
Our messaging balances mainstream, relatable emotional utility with deep technical trust. We are targeting three distinct archetypes:

Persona 1: The Group Chat Connector (Mainstream Social)
Who they are: Students, young professionals, and social planners who spend hours daily in group chats (WhatsApp, iMessage, Instagram DM).

Their Pain Point: Group chats easily devolve into chaos when planning a trip, picking a movie, or making decisions. Or conversations stall out and need a spark.

Why they care about Turtle: Turtle turns them into the "Group Chat Hero." They don't care about the underlying tech; they care that typing /quiz or /poll instantly drops a beautiful, fun, interactive widget into the chat without anyone needing to download a separate party game or survey app.

Persona 2: The Flow-State Pragmatist (Founders, Creators & Professionals)
Who they are: Busy individuals juggling emails, Slack, Notion, and messaging apps on the go.

Their Pain Point: Copying meeting notes from Notion to paste into an email, or switching apps to draft a quick summary while walking down the street, is slow and frustrating on a touchscreen.

Why they care about Turtle: Speed and uninterrupted workflow. They can type /notion add task or /summarize directly inside their email draft or Slack thread. Turtle acts as their mobile command line.

Persona 3: The Privacy-Conscious Developer (The Vanguard)
Who they are: Software engineers, open-source advocates, and productivity enthusiasts.

Their Pain Point: They hate Big Tech walled gardens (Apple Intelligence locking down iOS; Google locking down Android). They refuse to install third-party keyboards due to security and keylogging risks.

Why they care about Turtle: It is MIT-licensed open-source software built on the Model Context Protocol (MCP). They trust the local on-device inference architecture, and they want to build custom MCP servers to connect their own internal tools to their keyboard.

3. Global Brand & Aesthetic Guidelines
Vibe: Coastal Minimalism. Calm, fluid, reliable, and tactile. Opening this page should feel like stepping away from the chaotic tech noise and taking a breath of fresh ocean air.

Color Palette:

Sea-Foam Base (Background): Very pale, warm off-white, resembling dry white sand.

Bioluminescent Turquoise (Primary Action): A vibrant, glowing teal used for the / symbol, buttons, and interactive highlights.

Abyssal Navy (Text & Deep Section): A rich, dark slate/navy for typography, avoiding the harsh contrast of pure black.

Sea Glass (UI Components): Semi-transparent white with a strong background blur (backdrop-filter: blur(12px)), giving UI elements a frosted glass look.

Typography:

Headings & Copy: A clean, geometric sans-serif (Inter, Geist, or SF Pro) in Abyssal Navy.

Commands/Code: A crisp monospace font (Fira Code or JetBrains Mono) for /commands to anchor the technical precision.

Motion (The Tide): Slow, deliberate, fluid transitions. Elements float gently on scroll.

5. Micro-Interactions & Technical Handoff
Input Glow: When clicking any email input field, border transitions from subtle grey to glowing Bioluminescent Turquoise over 200ms.

Waitlist Feedback: Upon submitting an email, replace button text with an animated walking turtle icon for 1.5 seconds before displaying: "You're on the list 🐢".

Performance Target: To align with our technical SEO goals, image assets in the use-case cards must be WebP/AVIF format with lazy loading enabled, ensuring a near-instant Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) and zero layout shift.
